Australian Grains

Where Australia grows its grain, and where it sends it

Why Australian grain matters

Australia is one of the world's largest grain exporters, yet its harvests swing sharply with the weather, and most of what it grows leaves the country. Four grains are followed here: wheat, barley, oats and sorghum.

How to read this dashboard

The dashboard moves in four steps: where the grains are grown, how unpredictable the harvests are, which countries buy them, and how Australia compares with the rest of the world. Malaysia, a regular buyer, is picked out in the charts where it appears. Each section has its own Crop selector, which stays at the top of the screen as you scroll through that section.

02 Boom and Bust

What each state grows, and how much a harvest can change from one year to the next

Australian harvests depend heavily on rainfall, so output can jump or collapse within a single year. These four charts show what each state grows, the long history of national production, how large the swings in yield are, and how each state's total moved in the latest years.

What the data shows

At home, a handful of regions grow most of Australia's grain, and harvests rise and fall sharply with the weather. Abroad, almost all of it is sold to Asian buyers. Australia seldom leads the world in production or yield, but its role as an exporter is large and concentrated.

What it means for buyers like Malaysia

Malaysia is a mid-sized customer, usually ranking around Australia's top ten buyers. Because so much Australian grain goes to a few Asian markets, a poor harvest at home is felt across the buying countries too.
